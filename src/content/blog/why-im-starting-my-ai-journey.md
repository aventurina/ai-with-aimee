# Why I'm Starting My AI Learning Journey

If you're reading this, welcome. This is post #1 of what I hope becomes a real record of me learning AI from the ground up. Not because I'm trying to become an AI researcher or switch careers into machine learning, but because I keep noticing the same thing: AI is already reshaping how people work and live, and I don't want to just watch it happen from the sidelines.

## The moment it clicked for me

It started small. I began using ChatGPT for everyday things: generating decor and home organizing ideas, creating images, researching random topics I was curious about, even putting together a weekly menu plan and an exercise routine. None of it felt like "AI" in some big capital-letter sense. It just felt useful, in a way that kept saving me time and mental energy.

Then I took on a short film project for my volunteer work, and that's when things really shifted. I used Nano Banana, Veo, and ElevenLabs to bring it to life, and going through that process completely opened my eyes to how powerful AI actually is. What used to require a team, specialized skills, and a real budget, I was piecing together myself, learning as I went.

That experience pulled me further in, and I started using AI-assisted development at that same volunteer work. But somewhere in the middle of all that experimenting, I noticed a problem: I was moving fast and using these tools constantly, but I wasn't actually retaining much. I'd figure something out, use it, and then struggle to explain it a week later. If I wanted to build a real, deep understanding instead of just a collection of one-off wins, I needed to document the journey, not just use AI, but actually reflect on what I was learning as I went.

That's the real reason this blog exists. Not to perform expertise I don't have, but to give myself a place to look back and see how far I've come.

## What I actually want out of this

I'm not chasing a certificate or trying to become "technical" for its own sake. What I want is much more practical:

**At work**, I want to know when AI is genuinely the right tool for a task versus when it's just a shiny distraction. I want to get faster and better at the parts of my job that are repetitive or draining, so I can spend more energy on the parts that actually need a human brain.

**In my personal life**, I'm curious how much of the mental load I carry (planning, researching, decision-making, even just organizing my own thoughts) could be lighter with the right tools used the right way. Not to outsource my thinking, but to stop reinventing the wheel every time.

And honestly, I just want to understand *how this stuff works* well enough that I'm not intimidated by it anymore. Not at a PhD level. At a "I can hold my own in a conversation about this and make informed decisions" level.

## What this blog is going to be

This isn't going to be a polished authority blog where I pretend to have it all figured out. It's going to be closer to a public notebook:

- **What I'm learning**: concepts, tools, techniques, as I actually encounter them
- **What I try**: real experiments, not just theory. If I test a tool or build something small, I'll document what worked and what didn't
- **What surprises me**: including the stuff that turns out to be overhyped, and the stuff that turns out to be underhyped
- **What I get wrong**: because I will get things wrong, and pretending otherwise isn't useful to anyone, including future me

If you're on a similar path (curious about AI but not trying to become an engineer, just trying to actually *use* this moment well), I hope this ends up being useful to you too. And if you already know more than I do about any of this, I'd genuinely love to hear from you. Half the point of writing this in public is that I'd rather be corrected than stay comfortably wrong.

## What's next

I'm starting simple: getting a solid grasp of the basics, how large language models actually work under the hood, what makes a good prompt versus a bad one, and where the real limitations are versus the perceived ones. From there I'll branch into more hands-on stuff as I get comfortable.

Thanks for being here at post #1. Let's see where this goes.
